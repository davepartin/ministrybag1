# Growing Together: curriculum and software review

September 11, 2026. Prepared for Dave Partin. This is a dated review, not a replacement for curriculum-toc.csv or the project's direction files. No project source files were changed.

## Assessment

The strongest part of this project is its pastoral teaching pattern: a memorable story, Scripture, a reusable picture, personal reflection, and a concrete response to grace. Preserve that pattern. The work ahead is to make the whole curriculum as coherent as its strongest lessons and make the software dependable enough to hold personal discipleship work for years.

All 47 lesson JSON files exist. Thirty-two contain substantive material; fifteen are empty or brief placeholders. These are writing-inventory categories, not release approval. One substantive file, 203-08, teaches an older belonging lesson rather than its advertised discipleship topic and needs rebuilding. Therefore there are at least sixteen major writing/rebuilding assignments, plus revision and production work on the other lessons.

The 30 lessons in the 200 series have 25 substantive drafts and five empty/brief placeholders. The 201 series has the strongest consistency. All ten include a story or teaching illustration, a widget, three to five mid-lesson questions, and the standard three closing questions. The exact Lesson Review heading is missing in 201-10, although it has substantial closing synthesis.

## Fix these first

1. **Protect saved answers.** 202-05 and 202-09 both use `201-09-key`, `201-09-devos`, and `201-09-prayer`. 203-06 and 203-07 both use `203-07-key`, `203-07-devos`, and `203-07-prayer`. The renderer prefixes course ID, not the current lesson number, so each pair shares three actual storage keys and DOM IDs. One lesson can overwrite the other's answers. Introduce immutable lesson/question identities and a migration that preserves old values for review. Do not simply rename everything and strand existing answers. Already overwritten answers cannot be reconstructed from one shared value.
2. **Align title and teaching.** 203-06 is advertised in the TOC as Faith at Work and School, its JSON title says Answering Hard Questions, and its actual teaching is spiritual warfare. 203-08 is titled How to Disciple Others but contains an old Belonging lesson with 301 question IDs and group-session language. These require writing decisions, not title edits alone.
3. **Restore missing images.** The black-and-white and color files for the Last Supper in 202-10 and Jesus washing feet in 203-04 are absent. Four files total. Add asset-reference checks, including imageSequence arrays, to QA.
4. **Fix iframe initialization.** Browser logs report `Failed to execute 'observe' on 'MutationObserver': parameter 1 is not of type 'Node'`. The iframe onload handler observes `f.contentDocument.body` without checking that it exists. Guard document readiness, measure content robustly, and disconnect observers/listeners when widgets unmount. Verify collapse as well as expansion and viewport changes.
5. **Correct export scope.** The button says Email Lesson, but `exportAllResponses()` loops over every loaded lesson in the course. That is particularly inappropriate for personal prayers and reflections. Show an exact preview and explicit current-lesson/course scope. Do not rely on a very long mailto URL as the only portable backup.
6. **Surface save failures.** localStorage writes are unguarded. Add visible Saving/Saved/Could not save feedback, preserve unsaved text, and support a downloadable backup and restoration path. Login can remain undecided while this is fixed.
7. **Remove the client credential dependency.** An ESV API token is embedded in the browser source and repeated in project documentation. Treat it as exposed, plan replacement and server-side handling, and avoid copying it into reports or new code. No token was tested or changed in this review.

Relevant code: index.html around lines 2655 (course loading), 2895 (question IDs), 3033 (iframe setup), 3421 (saving), 3640 (email export). Question conflicts are visible in data/202-05.json:159, data/202-09.json:31, data/203-06.json:41, and data/203-07.json:121.

## Teaching review

**201-01 is a strong voice benchmark.** The running Father, receive-and-respond rhythm, and morning practice reinforce one burden. The bridge from the Father's robe to 201-02 makes the course feel authored. Keep the core length and warmth. Place the second reflection closer to the widget; consider turning one existing reflection into a brief explain-it-in-your-own-words prompt. Do not add another question merely to satisfy a checklist. The course orientation currently delays the story by several phone screens; make orientation collapsible after a learner has seen it.

**201-08 is a strong visual benchmark.** The story, diagram, and mercy application belong together. Improve the definition of justification: suggest, for Dave's review, 'God declares you righteous because of Jesus, received by faith.' Christ paying the debt explains its basis; 'your punishment is paid' alone is incomplete as its definition. Keep the cycle clearly about grace received becoming mercy extended, so a learner does not infer that showing mercy earns their original acceptance. Move 8.2 immediately after the diagram if the intent is to process the experience before the extended explanation. The current sequence places five explanatory steps between the widget and 8.2. The opening also defines all four terms before the story; a story-first edit would better match the project's stated approach.

**201-03 needs precise Trinity language.** Replace 'the only scene ... where the whole Trinity is visible at once' with 'a scene where all three persons are revealed together.' The Father speaks; the passage does not describe Him as visible. Label the pillar image as an analogy rather than saying the physical pillar now lives in the learner. Separate testing guidance by Scripture and wise counsel from simply feeling settled peace. Source: [Matthew 3:16-17](https://www.esv.org/verses/Matthew%2B3%3A16%E2%80%9317/).

**201-04 needs a focused metaphor and a shorter core.** The soil, roots, vine, house, and rock images stack up across roughly 2,500 words of lesson blocks, including questions and Scripture but excluding widget text. Keep the five practices and root picture; reduce repeated explanation and move optional depth out of the main reading. In Jesus' explanation of the sower, good soil describes receptive hearers. 'Jesus is the Good Soil' should not be presented as that parable's direct meaning or as an established title without support. 'A heart rooted in Jesus' preserves the pastoral goal more clearly. Source: [Luke 8:11-15](https://www.esv.org/verses/Lk%208%3A11-15/).

**201-05 through 201-07 form a useful progression.** Map, storyline, and study method build on one another. 201-06 question 6.4 is especially valuable: it asks the learner to explain the truth to someone else. Reuse that kind of retrieval across the curriculum. In 201-06, qualify the claim that nearly every Bible story follows all four movements; use the framework to locate stories within God's larger story without forcing every account into a happy ending. In 201-07, keep the timeline as a supporting aid and make Observe, Interpret, Apply the main take-away visual. The current widget illustrates chronology rather than the central study method.

**201-09 needs correction and reduction.** It says 'You are the vine. Jesus is the Vinedresser.' John 15 identifies Jesus as the vine, His Father as the vinedresser, and disciples as branches. Correct both prose and any corresponding widget language. Remove or substantiate the Bonhoeffer execution anecdote; the lesson itself admits uncertainty, which weakens trust. At roughly 2,300 block words plus its widget and eight written questions, it is a likely time-budget outlier. Keep the nine qualities, but let the widget carry some descriptions rather than repeating them at length. Source: [John 15:1,5](https://www.esv.org/verses/John%2015%3A1%2C%205/).

**201-10 closes the arc well.** The two brothers let repentance address religious pride as well as obvious rebellion. Preserve that. Under the Believe side of the Learning Circle, make trusting Christ's promise explicit before planning and obeying. Planning should grow from faith rather than becoming its definition. Verify and document attribution/reuse terms for the adapted tool. Make the closing review easy to retrieve in a discussion view.

**Audit quotations in widgets as carefully as lesson text.** The Grace and Mercy widget labels Romans 5:9 as ESV while using different wording and inserting explanatory parentheticals into the quotation. It also uses NIV elsewhere. Use exact translation text, clearly labeled excerpts, or explicitly marked paraphrases. Put explanatory glosses outside the quote. Source: [Romans 5:8-9](https://www.esv.org/Romans%2B5%3A8%E2%80%939%3B1%2BJohn%2B4%3A10/).

For suffering, family, purity, forgiveness, and generosity lessons, review applications with actual pastoral situations in mind. Avoid making faithful people feel that grief, slow healing, limited money, or necessary boundaries prove spiritual failure. Keep forgiveness distinct from automatically restored trust or unrestricted access. These are editorial priorities, not conclusions that every current lesson mishandles them.

## Images and widgets

The inspected running-Father, king's-mercy, and five-gardeners images are emotionally legible and share a recognizable charcoal style. Keep that visual identity. Their color highlights are restrained; do not make dramatic animation the point. The running-Father alt text describes an embrace even though the image shows him running toward a distant son; align descriptions with the actual picture. A simple logo at phone scale would be clearer than the current dense lettering inside three circles.

Performance needs attention. The 101 lesson references total about 12.9 MiB of existing source image files; the 201 references total 15.0 MiB, excluding additional widget assets and course-shell logos. These are file-size inventories, not measured network transfers. Loading the 201 course creates ten iframes and 34 image elements even when one lesson is shown. Render the active lesson, defer the next lesson until needed, use responsive compressed assets, and reserve image dimensions. Preserve originals separately. Test one representative charcoal image before batch conversion so cross-hatching remains sharp.

The automatic Date.now cache busting is an explicit current project rule. For production, propose changing that rule to deployment/content versioning so repeat visits can reuse assets. Do not silently change it during unrelated lesson editing.

There are 19 standalone widget HTML files; 17 are directly referenced by lesson widget blocks. apest-rating and three-circles-video are not directly referenced that way, but may have other links. Do not delete them without a dependency search. Self-contained widgets are a sensible starting architecture. Their controls, fonts, height rules, and accessibility should come from one shared shell while their teaching graphics remain distinct.

Adopt a small set of tool types: guided diagram, reflection/assessment, and practical exercise. Give each an identifiable title, consistent Begin/Back/Next/Restart controls, keyboard access, visible focus, a readable static summary, and an appropriate print version. Add 'Show whole diagram' for repeat use. The Grace widget measured 651 pixels high on a 390-pixel phone, above the widget guide's approximate 620-pixel target; its final state was readable, but title, content, and controls need testing together on smaller screens. Never solve overflow by clipping teaching text.

Accessibility work is concrete: iframe titles are missing; some diagram click targets and checklist tiles lack equivalent keyboard semantics. Use generous touch areas, clear contrast for active content, reduced-motion behavior, and test text zoom. Target WCAG 2.2 AA; 44-pixel controls are a sensible product target, while the AA minimum target-size criterion is 24 pixels subject to its exceptions. Source: [W3C target-size guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html).

## The highest-value product improvement

Keep one lesson and offer two presentations of it: Read and Discuss.

Read preserves the existing story, teaching, tool, and written reflections. Discuss immediately surfaces the one-sentence lesson truth, full diagram, the learner's key insight, this week's chosen step, and prayer request. A small 'Last time' area can help revisit the prior commitment. Optional teaching notes can support a discipler without lengthening every learner's reading.

Use the existing closing reflections as the basis. Do not create a second curriculum or duplicate every answer. Private responses should remain private until the learner deliberately chooses what to share. A church administrator should not automatically receive access to personal prayers.

Laptop preparation followed by phone conversation requires a real transfer or synchronization story. localStorage currently saves only in that browser on that device. Backend choice can wait, but define the required behavior now: portable backup, stable IDs, versioned content, explicit sharing, eventual cross-device sync, and conflict handling. The existing Supabase document is a proposal; it does not override Dave's undecided choice.

This plan produces fifteen new/expanded lessons plus the 203-08 rebuild while improving the existing 31 relevant drafts. The leadership-writing pace in weeks 7-10 is ambitious. If actual pastoral review capacity cannot support it, protect a polished 37-lesson 100/200 release and give 301 a separate release date. Do not lower the approval standard to make the file count look finished.

Recruit four to six learner/discipler pairs with varied reading confidence and at least one pastor from another church. They cannot complete a 47-week journey within twelve weeks; assign overlapping lesson samples to test the whole curriculum, while a smaller group follows a continuous arc. Ask whether they can explain the main idea without rereading, use the picture, name and attempt one step, and retrieve their work during a meeting. Pilot learning and spiritual practice, not just whether people liked the page.

## Definition of ready

A lesson is ready when Dave approves its Scripture and theology; a learner can explain its one big truth; the story and tool reinforce the same point; there is one doable practice; reading, writing, and conversation fit the intended rhythm in observed use; and the software preserves and retrieves the person's work. Broken assets, unverified quotations, missing tools, and placeholder sections fail readiness even if the JSON parses.

For wide free distribution, add a plain doctrinal-position page, a short church/discipler guide, a clear statement of allowed reuse for original materials, and a checked attribution/reuse record for borrowed tools, images, and Scripture. Make local church naming configurable; replace NC-specific destination language in the shared core. Avoid adding church administration, gamification, and a large dashboard before the learning experience is dependable.

## Working agreement proposed

Dave owns theology, pastoral voice, and final approval. The assistant supplies the first edit, code changes, image preparation, widget consistency, automated checks, and a concise account of what changed and why. Work in small complete batches: approve one lesson's burden, revise it, test it on a phone, and record its approval before moving it to ready. Keep public-readiness status in structured data so unfinished lessons do not appear equally complete beside approved ones.

The next concrete work package should fix the answer collisions with migration protection, the four missing image references, iframe initialization, and the misleading export action. Then produce one complete Read/Discuss prototype using 201-08 before applying the pattern across the curriculum.

## Proposed learner manuscript

### Journey recap

In the last lesson, you saw that worship is a response to God's worth. That response reaches into your calendar, your skills, and your possessions. Generosity begins with receiving what God gives, then learning to use it for His purposes.

### Two Small Coins

Jesus sat across from the temple offering box and watched people give. Many rich people put in large amounts. Then a poor widow came and put in two small copper coins, worth about a penny.

Jesus called His disciples to notice her. Her gift was small beside the others, but He knew what it cost her. He said she had put in more than those giving from their abundance, because she had given all she had to live on (Mark 12:41-44).

Jesus saw the woman as well as the coins. Her poverty did not make her invisible or her gift unimportant. The passage does not say that she became wealthy afterward. It shows that Jesus sees what others may overlook.

### A Gift Before a Response

Generosity starts with what Jesus has already given you. Paul begins his teaching about giving with this reminder:

> For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor, so that you by his poverty might become rich.
>
> 2 Corinthians 8:9, ESV

Those riches are found in belonging to Jesus: forgiveness, adoption into God's family, and life with Him. This is not a promise that following Him will fill your bank account. He gave Himself to bring you to God, and you cannot buy that gift or repay it. You can receive His grace and let it shape what you do with what He has placed in your hands.

**Proposed question 7.1:** How has God's generosity shaped the way you think about what you have?

### What Is a Steward?

A steward is someone trusted to care for what belongs to another person. God owns what He places in your hands, and He asks you to use it for His purposes. Stewardship means caring faithfully for His gifts, including the part you share and the part you use for daily responsibilities.

> The earth is the LORD's and the fullness thereof, the world and those who dwell therein.
>
> Psalm 24:1, ESV

Your time, abilities, money, home, and possessions are gifts entrusted to you. Generosity grows from that understanding. You can ask, What would faithful care look like with what God has given me today?

That includes feeding your family, keeping a promise, resting, paying what you owe, and helping someone in need. God's purposes reach into ordinary life. Caring for what He gives and sharing it with others belong together.

### Time, Talents, Treasure

**God owns it all: your time, talents, and treasure.**

[Editorial insertion point: Gemini's three-T stool. This bracketed instruction is not learner prose.]

Your **time** is the life you have to spend. You can give someone your attention, visit a lonely person, or make room for an unhurried conversation. Work, rest, and caring for your household also need faithful attention. Generosity asks where love can make room within your real responsibilities.

Your **talents** are abilities you can put to use. You may cook, repair things, encourage a child, organize a task, or listen patiently. Some skills come easily and others grow through practice. You do not have to wait until you are exceptional before using what you can do to help.

Your **treasure** includes money and possessions. A gift to your church, help with a meal, or a tool lent to a neighbor can put what you have to work for someone else's good. Giving money matters, and so does how you care for the resources you keep.

The three words describe one life. Giving money does not replace caring attention, and sharing a skill does not make the rest of your life off limits to God. You can ask Him to grow your generosity across all three, starting with a realistic next step.

**Proposed question 7.2:** Which of your time, talents, or treasure do you find hardest to entrust to God?

### Faithful with What You Have

Jesus said, "One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much" (Luke 16:10). Begin with what is in your hands. Keep a small commitment, use a skill to help, or share an amount that fits your circumstances. Faithfulness develops as you practice it.

God may entrust you with greater responsibility, but generosity is not a bargain for more money. Some deeply faithful believers live with very little. In 2 Corinthians 8:2-4, Paul describes churches whose generosity grew amid severe trouble and deep poverty. Their faithfulness was not measured by wealth.

Nor should someone use the widow's story to pressure you into unsafe giving or neglecting people who depend on you. Scripture calls for willingness according to what a person has, not what they do not have (2 Corinthians 8:12). You can seek wise help as you learn to care and share faithfully.

### Give Gladly

> Each one must give as he has decided in his heart, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver.
>
> 2 Corinthians 9:7, ESV

You may notice fear, reluctance, or a desire to be noticed when you give. Bring that honestly to God. He has already welcomed you through Jesus. Ask Him to help you care about another person's good and take a willing step within what He has entrusted to you.

**Proposed question 7.3:** What is one willing step of generosity you could take this week?

### A Picture You Can Pass On

Remember the three Ts: time, talents, treasure. You can sketch three Ts into a stool and use the picture to explain that your whole life belongs to God. The goal is not equal amounts of giving in each area. It is learning to hold each part of life open to Him.

**Proposed question 7.4:** How would you explain whole-life stewardship to a friend using time, talents, and treasure?

### This Week's Practice

Choose one specific act of generosity with your time, talents, or treasure. Name who it will help and when you will do it. Start small enough to follow through, and offer it as a response to God's grace rather than a way to earn His love.

Then notice whether one of the other areas has stayed closed to Him. Bring that to God in prayer. You do not have to remake your whole life in a week; you are learning to be faithful with what He has given you.

### Lesson 7 Review

God's generosity comes first: Jesus gave Himself to bring you into His family. A steward cares for what belongs to another, and God has entrusted you with **time, talents, and treasure**. You can care for those gifts and share them gladly, whether you have much or little. Begin with what is in your hands and let your whole life respond to His grace.

In the next lesson, you will see how God's covenant love shapes your closest relationships.

## Dave's direction

The final lesson should teach genuine love, help learners name their growth, and warmly send them to share Jesus and make disciples who make disciples. The 203 course moves from personal ministry through BLESS, everyday witness, standing firm, discipling others, and partners in the mission. Its last lesson should carry that outward purpose into ordinary life.

The bookend is 201-01's loving Father pursuing His wayward son and 203-10's followers carrying that same love toward others, even enemies. Romans 12 describes the character of a life being shaped by Jesus. It is a demanding passage, but we grow into it through relationship with Jesus, who stays with us and helps us by His Spirit. We respond to love already received. One of the joys of following Christ is giving that life away to someone else.

Elisha's servant needed opened eyes to see God's presence and protection. Israel's king then needed to respond to enemies with hospitality instead of retaliation. Connect seeing God's work with taking the next loving step. Do not promise that every enemy becomes a friend or that every soldier's heart changed. The passage says the raids stopped; it does not say all war permanently ended. The current story's closing phrase, "A war ended at a dinner table," deserves an accuracy check when implementing this packet.

**Purpose:** Help a learner explore Romans 12:9-21 in short, meaningful groups rather than face one intimidating paragraph. The life of Jesus is growing in how we treat others. This is a joyful course finale, grounded in received mercy and Jesus' continuing presence.

**One visual:** A modest shared table, drawn simply in the series' Studio Wash style. Keep the camera, table, people, and proportions fixed. On each learner-controlled step, small acts of love bring the same scene to life. Do not turn it into unrelated mini-scenes or a dense infographic. Make the finished scene beautiful and readable before animating it.

**Four teaching groups:**

1. **Love sincerely**, Romans 12:9-10: genuine love, rejecting evil, holding to good, affection, honor. Let attention and open gestures establish care between people.
2. **Stay close to God**, verses 11-12: serving the Lord, hope, patience, prayer. Show a quiet shared pause, without suggesting that a special pose measures someone's faith.
3. **Make room for others**, verses 13, 15-16: sharing resources, hospitality, sharing joy and grief, humility, harmony. Someone makes room, another shares bread, and another comes alongside a hurting person.
4. **Love through conflict**, verses 14, 17-21: blessing persecutors, refusing retaliation, seeking peace, leaving judgment with God, feeding enemies, overcoming evil with good. The unfamiliar person who stood outside now has a place at the table. Convey welcome without inventing a guaranteed conversion or reconciliation.

**The memorable finish:** The final composition reveals that genuine love has made room for someone the group might have excluded. Echo Elisha's feast and the Father's welcome. Keep the people human and dignified, with no villain caricature, forced handshake, victory celebration, or score.

**Scripture:** Use the exact current ESV wording. The group labels are explanatory labels, not Bible quotations. Account for the whole passage, including the difficult lines about evil, peace, vengeance, and burning coals; do not quietly omit them to make the list cheerful. Do not describe the grouping as Paul's own four-part outline. A verse may appear under its thematic group with its correct reference, even when that changes presentation order. Loving an enemy does not require renewed access or staying in danger.

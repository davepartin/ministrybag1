1
Turn experience into clarity

Think

This is the lowest-friction category and the best place to start if you've never tried any of this. It doesn't require building anything — just talking.

Screenshota voice memo turning into a clean call summary

The post-call voice dump weekly move

Right after a call with a planter, open the app and just talk. It writes a clean summary. Drop it into your note on that planter. Do this every week and your quarterly report is basically already written.

I just got off a call with a planter. I'm going to talk and you turn it into a clean summary I can save: who I talked to, where they are, what we covered, and the next step. Here it is: [talk or type]

Build a talk from a rough idea

The same move that built the "From Dream to Done" lunch talk.

I want to give a short talk to planters on [topic]. Here's my rough idea: [paste your thoughts]. Help me turn this into a tight, 5-7 minute talk with a clear structure and a few lines worth underlining.

"What am I forgetting?"

Use it as a second brain before a meeting or a pipeline step.

I have a pre-application meeting with a potential planter this week. What are good questions to discern calling, family health, doctrine, and readiness? Keep it to one page.

Confusing form or screenshot explained

Here's a screenshot of a form/document I don't fully understand. Explain what it's asking for in plain language, and tell me what I actually need to fill in or do. [attach/paste]

One reminder

It can sound confident and still be wrong. You're always the editor — treat it like a sharp intern, not like Scripture.

Turn ideas into things

Make

This is where it stops feeling like a smarter search engine and starts feeling like a shop. You describe what you want, and it builds it with you — no developer required.

A study Bible built for you, not sold to you Dave's story

A personal PDF study Bible and sermon journal, one book at a time: left column for the sermon point, summary, and illustration; middle column with the WEB Bible passage; right column left blank for handwritten notes with an Apple Pencil. Nobody was ever going to build this for you — it took an afternoon.

Help me design a personal study Bible PDF for [book of the Bible]. Three columns: left for my sermon points/summary/illustrations, middle for the Bible text, right column left blank for handwritten notes. Walk me through building it one book at a time.

Turn a document into a clean one-page handout

Take this content and make it a clean, one-page handout a planter could print: clear headings, short sections, and a checklist at the bottom. [paste content]

Slides or teaching visuals

Help me build a short slide deck for [talk/topic]. Big, minimal slides — one idea per slide, my notes carry the detail. Give me the structure first, then we'll fill it in.
3
Nobody falls through the cracks

Organize

You don't need to become an organized person. You need one dashboard that tells you who's falling through the cracks.

Screenshotthe planter dashboard — status, last contact, next step

The 30-day flag Dave's story

Every planter gets a note. Every note has a status and a next step. Once a week, one dashboard tells you who you haven't contacted in 30 days. The tracking is automatic once it's set up — you don't have to be naturally organized.

Help me build a simple spreadsheet to track my church planters. Columns for name, stage (potential, candidate, funded, aged-out), sending church, last contact, and next step. Then help me add a rule that flags anyone I haven't contacted in 30 days.

Hand it your current mess

You don't start from scratch — you start from what you already have.

Here's how I currently keep up with my planters: [describe your messy system]. Here's what I wish it did: [describe]. Help me design a simpler, repeatable way to do this, step by step.

A repeatable weekly workflow

Help me build a simple weekly routine with AI: review my planters, draft any follow-up emails, and update my tracker. Give me a short checklist I can run every week.
4
The turn

Share

This may be the most important category, because it's not really about AI. A few weeks ago the planters got a talk called "From Dream to Done" — seven moves that take an idea to reality.

  1. Dream it — Vision. Name the win in one sentence.
  2. Test it — Prayer. Take it to God before the whiteboard.
  3. Weigh it — Wisdom. Bring in wise people and real information.
  4. Divide it — Plan. Cut it into pieces small enough to start Monday.
  5. Do it — Action. Take the first real step this week.
  6. Sharpen it — Review. Ask honestly, did this work.
  7. Share it — Multiply. Don't hoard it — hand it to the next guy starting from scratch.

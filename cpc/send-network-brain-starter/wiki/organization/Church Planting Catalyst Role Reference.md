# Church Planting Catalyst Role Reference

> A single consolidated reference for what a Church Planting Catalyst does. Adapt the region-specific details (associations, seminary partners, housing) to your context.

## One-Sentence Summary

A Church Planting Catalyst helps **find, assess, care for, equip, connect, and mobilize planters and churches so new gospel-centered churches are planted and strengthened.**

## The Two Sides of the Role

1. **Planter-facing:** Walk individual planters from first contact through assessment, endorsement, care, equipping, and long-term health.
2. **Church-facing:** Move churches along a mobilization pathway from basic cooperation toward becoming sending and multiplying churches.

Both are held together by partnership and operations work: associations, the state convention, NAMB systems, and the regional team.

---

## Side 1: Planter-Facing Work (PACE)

See `Send KC Catalyze Planters PACE.md` for the full framework. In summary:

- **Prepare:** identify potential planters; respond promptly to leads and cold contacts; give a regional overview; help discern calling, character, family health, and readiness; explain the full Send Network process; confirm BF&M 2000 and the NAMB Code of Conduct; walk the 9 Essentials; review the Planter Benefits Package.
- **Assess:** conduct the pre-application meeting; verify eligibility; notify NAMB to send the official application link; coordinate pre-assessment diagnostics (CPIA, PPQ, 360 references, DISC, theology questionnaire, Ministry Safe); prepare planter and spouse for the 2-day assessment retreat; discuss final reports honestly. Outcomes: **Ready to Plant**, **Further Development**, or **Redirect**.
- **Care:** ongoing pastoral care, encouragement, prayer, and follow-up; monthly planter lunches; annual appreciation and family events; spouse care; orientation attendance.
- **Equip:** Send Network Training; annual planter retreat; quarterly reports with assigned planters (typically 10–15 per CPC); help build planting/support teams, budgets, and timelines; clarify startup funds; set up sending-church coaching; guide the church from receiving plant toward becoming a sending church.

---

## Side 2: Church-Facing Work (Mobilization)

See `Send KC Church Mobilization Pathway.md`. The CPC helps existing churches take their next faithful step:

- Build relationships with pastors and potential sending churches.
- Use the five-level pathway as a shared conversation map: help a pastor see their current level and one concrete next step.
- Promote and coordinate **Sending Labs**, **Residency Builders**, **Learning Communities**, and **Multiplier Events**.
- Help churches identify, develop, send, support, and care for their own planters.
- Plan an annual citywide church planting gathering.

---

## Side 3: Partnerships and Operations

- **Associations and convention:** build relationships with local association leaders (Directors of Mission / Associational Missional Strategists), the state convention, and the regional Send Network director. Adapt these to your region.
- **Seminary partners:** connect with a nearby seminary for planter identification and development.
- **Housing:** where NAMB housing exists, coordinate occupancy with the housing liaison.
- **Administrative systems (NAMB platforms):** MyNAMB.net, MAP (Missionary Admin Platform), the Assessment Platform, the Missionary Dashboard, Workday (expenses), and the MyPath portal (applications).
- **Record-keeping:** track all planters through dashboards and check-ins; maintain pipeline data (potential → candidate → funded → aged-out); keep records current so planters and churches do not fall through the cracks.

---

## The 12-Step Endorsement Process

**Phase 1: Discovery and Interest** — 1) Connection, 2) Interest Form at namb.net, 3) Relationship building with local field staff (CPC).
**Phase 2: Pre-Application** — 4) Pre-application meeting, 5) Document/eligibility review, 6) Launch application (CPC notifies NAMB to send the link).
**Phase 3: Application and Approval** — 7) Submission via MyPath (background checks + references), 8) Pre-assessment diagnostics, 9) Approval for assessment.
**Phase 4: Assessment and Care** — 10) 2-day assessment retreat, 11) The outcome (Ready to Plant / Further Development / Redirect), 12) Long-term support.

## Compliance and Doctrine

Every planter affirms and maintains **Baptist Faith and Message 2000** compliance, the **NAMB Code of Conduct** (integrity, tithing, abstinence standards, relational boundaries, missions giving), SBC membership in good standing, and Ministry Safe child-abuse-prevention training.

---

## Honest Tensions in the Role

- **Relational vs. administrative:** the role demands deep relational investment AND rigorous systems. One without the other fails.
- **Volume vs. depth:** always more to do than time allows.
- **Pastoral vs. evaluative:** the CPC is a pastor to planters AND the person who walks them through assessment.
- **Encouragement vs. clarity:** some planters need encouragement, some need hard truth. Deliver both with love.

*This is exactly where a well-kept vault helps: it carries the administrative load so the CPC has more of themselves left for the relational, pastoral, discerning work only a person can do.*

  4. Do the Level 1 step below today. That is it. You are started.

The one habit that makes AI useful

Be specific and give context. Do not say "write an email." Say who it is for, what it needs to do, how long, and what tone. The more you tell it, the better it gets. Treat it like a sharp new assistant who is fast but needs clear direction.

Ask a real work question try first

Instead of digging through five links, just ask and then read what it gives you.

I'm a church planting catalyst with Send Network. Explain the difference between a cooperating, supporting, and sending church in simple terms I could use with a pastor over coffee.

Turn dense material into plain words

Paste a long NAMB, denominational, or policy document and ask for the short version.

Here is a document. Summarize it in plain language in under 150 words, then list the 3 things I actually need to do. [paste the text]

Great for onboarding a new planter to a process without making them read the whole packet.

"What am I forgetting?"

Use it as a second brain before a meeting or a step in the pipeline.

I have a pre-application meeting with a potential planter this week. What are good questions to discern calling, family health, doctrine, and readiness? Keep it to one page.

One reminder

It can sound confident and still be wrong. You are always the editor. Trust it like a sharp intern, not like Scripture. Read what it gives you before you use it.

Off the phone → into the notes Dave's weekly move

The simplest habit that keeps a planter from falling through the cracks.

  1. Talk. Right after a call, open the app and just talk it out.
  2. Summarize. It writes a clean summary as a document.
  3. Save. Drop it into your note on that planter so you remember next time.
I just got off a call with a planter. I'm going to talk and you turn it into a clean summary I can save: who I talked to, where they are, what we covered, and the next step. Here it is: [talk or type]

Do this all quarter and your quarterly report almost writes itself.

Hand it your workflow the big one

You do not start from scratch. You start from your current mess and let it help you shape it.

Here is how I currently keep up with my planters: [describe your messy system]. Here is what I wish it did: [describe]. Help me design a simpler, repeatable way to do this, step by step.

This works in a tool like Claude or ChatGPT. Just talk it through like you would with an assistant.

Turn a document into a clean one-page PDF

One guardrail

We handle real people

These tools are safe for everyday drafting, summarizing, and building. Just be wise about what you paste in, because we track real people and sometimes hard details.

Rule of thumb: if you wouldn't put it on a postcard, think before you paste it.

Fine to use it for

Cleaning up emails, summarizing your own call notes, drafting announcements, building trackers and handouts, sermon and teaching prep, learning a process.

Be careful with

Truly sensitive or confidential details: marriage struggles, assessment results, immigration status, integrity or legal matters, and full personal identifiers. Keep names and private details out of it, or use only the accounts our team approves for sensitive work.

A quick habit

When in doubt, swap real names for "the planter" and describe the situation generally. You still get the help, without exposing a person's private story.
